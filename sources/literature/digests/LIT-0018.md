## KEY CLAIMS + LOCATORS
- The study illuminates the pivotal role of institutional segregation in shaping the long-term reproduction of elite families by examining patterns of mobility among Bannermen and Han Chinese during the Qing era. [abstract]
- The Qing state, through preferential policies favoring Bannermen, enabled the enduring maintenance of Bannermen's elevated social status across generations. [abstract]
- While education could serve as a powerful equalizer in the short term, institutionalized privileges and barriers generated persistent disparities over time. [abstract]
- Bannermen exhibited lower levels of multigenerational mobility compared to their Han Chinese counterparts, which was attributable to the institutional advantages granted to Bannermen in attaining and retaining high social standing. [abstract]
- Han Chinese faced intense competition within the examination and bureaucratic systems, resulting in a greater likelihood of downward mobility across generations. [abstract]
- Despite these differences, both groups demonstrated comparable levels of intergenerational relative mobility, with correlation coefficients around 0.4. [abstract]
- This similarity may be explained by adaptive strategies among Han families, such as substantial investment in education and the formation of advantageous marriage alliances, which increased their chances of upward mobility even in the face of institutional barriers. [abstract]
- The main results remain robust after reweighting Han Chinese families to match the national geographic and temporal distribution of degree holders and after excluding Jiangnan. [abstract]
