## THESIS
The paper introduces a new source — a dataset constructed from Tongguanlu rosters of officials — for the study of the kin networks, qualifications, and careers of officials in China in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, and uses it to examine the relationship between the degrees officials held and the degrees held by their kin.

## PRIMARY MATERIALS USED
- Tongguanlu rosters of officials, including their resumes, degree or other qualifications, and rosters of their kin; stated as the basis for a newly constructed dataset.
- The dataset covers the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

## KEY CLAIMS + LOCATORS
- The paper introduces a new source for the study of the kin networks, qualifications, and careers of officials in China in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries: a dataset constructed from Tongguanlu rosters of officials that include their resumes, degree or other qualifications, and rosters of their kin. [abstract]
- In contrast with other sources that have been used to study the social origins of holders of high examination degrees including national Jinshi, provincial Juren, and exam Gongsheng, Tongguanlu include holders of less prestigious purchased degrees and prefectural Shengyuan exam degrees, who accounted for a large share of officials, especially local ones, in the nineteenth century. [abstract]
- Information about kin includes not only the names and degrees held by paternal kin but also those of maternal kin and affines. [abstract]
- Using this dataset to examine the relationship between the degrees officials held and the degrees held by their kin, the authors find that officials who held purchased degrees and low-level Shengyuan examination degrees were less likely than holders of higher degrees to have other kin who held degrees. [abstract]
- The authors also find that officials with regular and expectant appointments were more likely to have kin with degrees than officials with acting appointments. [abstract]
