## THESIS
The paper examines intergenerational mobility across social classes during the late Qing dynasty using a data set from Liaoning province in Northeast China, and identifies two distinct epochs with markedly different mobility dynamics.

## PRIMARY MATERIALS USED
- A data set from Liaoning province in Northeast China, described in the abstract as "a remarkable data set"; the abstract does not name the underlying register, archive, or collection from which the data set is built.

## METHOD / INFERENCE
The abstract states the analysis employs Markov-chain measures and two alternative mobility concepts: the persistence of class origin across generations and intergenerational class movement. No further specification of the data set's construction or estimation is given in the abstract.

## KEY CLAIMS + LOCATORS
- The paper examines intergenerational mobility across social classes during the late Qing dynasty employing a data set from Liaoning province in Northeast China. [abstract]
- Two distinct epochs with markedly different mobility dynamics are identified. [abstract]
- Before 1850, mobility patterns exhibited stability and convergence toward a steady state. [abstract]
- The second epoch, beginning around 1850, was characterized by unstable intergenerational class dynamics that persisted until the dynasty's collapse. [abstract]
- The transition between epochs coincides with the Opium Wars and Taiping Rebellion, demonstrating how the footprints of major crises in the late Qing era can be traced in mobility dynamics. [abstract]
- Using Markov-chain measures and two alternative mobility concepts — the persistence of class origin across generations and intergenerational class movement — the authors document that intergenerational mobility increased over the period. [abstract]
- The aggregate increase in intergenerational mobility masked a decline in upward mobility alongside a rise in downward mobility — disparate patterns that resonate with broader theories of political instability. [abstract]
