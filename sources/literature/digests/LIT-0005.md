## THESIS
The paper introduces a generic machine learning-based pipeline for the nominative linkage of records within and across large-scale Chinese historical datasets, arguing that methods built for attributes recorded in phonetic alphabets are of limited usefulness for datasets recorded in Chinese characters.

## PRIMARY MATERIALS USED
- Career records of officials in the China Government Employee Database-Qing Jinshenlu (CGED-Q JSL), used as the test case for the pipeline.
- Another (unnamed) database and a cross-database linkage task, used to validate generalizability.

## METHOD / INFERENCE
The pipeline is described as integrating three components: (1) stroke-based character embeddings for efficient blocking, (2) supervised classification with active learning for record matching, and (3) graph-based clustering for final linkage. The abstract states the pipeline addresses character variations, incomplete data, and scalability issues specific to historical datasets in which names and other attributes are recorded with Chinese characters, and notes the approach is potentially relevant not only for China but for Korea, Japan, and Vietnam.

## KEY CLAIMS + LOCATORS
- The paper introduces a generic machine learning-based pipeline for nominative linkage of records within and across large-scale Chinese historical datasets. [abstract]
- The pipeline addresses key challenges including character variations, incomplete data, and scalability issues specific to historical datasets recorded with Chinese characters. [abstract]
- Techniques developed for attributes recorded in phonetic alphabets are of limited usefulness for Chinese characters, because homonyms are common and characters similar enough in appearance to be mistaken for each other may sound completely different. [abstract]
- The approach integrates stroke-based character embeddings for efficient blocking, supervised classification with active learning for record matching, and graph-based clustering for final linkage. [abstract]
- Using career records of officials in the CGED-Q JSL as a test case, the pipeline achieves improved linkage quality compared to standard probabilistic methods, with substantially longer linked sequences of career records and fewer aberrant transitions. [abstract]
- The pipeline was also successfully applied to another database and to a cross-database linkage task, to validate generalizability. [abstract]
- By minimizing the need for manual tuning, the pipeline offers a more accessible and effective solution for Chinese historical data linkage. [abstract]
